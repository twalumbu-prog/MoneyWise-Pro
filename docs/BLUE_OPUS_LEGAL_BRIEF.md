# Blue Opus share offers: brief for the company's lawyer

Prepared by the engineering team. **This is not legal advice and the agreement wording is a draft written by us, not by a lawyer.** Nothing in this feature can take a person's money until you (the lawyer) have approved the wording and Blue Opus records that approval in the system.

## What the feature does

Blue Opus Software is a private company (not listed). It wants to raise **K1,500,000 for 20%**, i.e. a **K7,500,000 post-money valuation** (figures from the investor deck, Nov 2026), from people it chooses to approach.

- The offer lives inside the MoneyWise app (Invest section). **Only invited people can see it.** Everyone else sees a page that says "This is a private offer" and asks for an invitation code. No valuation, figures or agreement text are shown or sent to them.
- Blue Opus (a platform super-admin) creates an **invitation for a named person**: a one-time code (`BO-XXXXXXXX`), optionally with an expiry date. The person enters it (or opens the link with the code) and that one account gets access. A code works once, for one account. Blue Opus can revoke it at any time, and can cap the number of investors.
- The invited person reads the terms, chooses an amount (minimum K1,000, optionally a per-person maximum and a round cap), ticks four declarations, types their full name to sign, and submits an **offer**. Signing is an offer, not a purchase.
- Blue Opus reviews every offer. It can **accept (countersign)**, **mark paid** or **decline**, with a note. The person is notified. **The system does not take or move any money**; Blue Opus tells the person how to pay.
- Ownership % = amount / post-money valuation, **fixed at signing** and stored with the offer (later changes to the valuation do not change a signed offer).
- Offers are **blocked until the lawyer's approval of the current wording version is recorded** (name of the lawyer or firm, date). Any change to the wording is a new version and is blocked until approved again.

## What we store for each offer

Name, email, phone (as typed), amount, valuation and percentage at signing, the wording version accepted, the typed signature and its timestamp and IP address, the four declarations and the time, the invitation used, status and Blue Opus's note.

## The four declarations the investor ticks

1. Blue Opus invited me personally to make this offer.
2. I am 18 or older.
3. I can afford to lose all of this money.
4. I am deciding for myself and have had the chance to take my own advice.

## Questions we need you to answer

1. **Is this lawful as designed?** A private company inviting a limited, named group (no advertising) to subscribe for shares: what exemption or limit applies under the Companies Act and the Securities Act, and what is the maximum number of investors, if any? Does the **Securities and Exchange Commission** need to be told or approve anything? Is regulated **crowdfunding** available?
2. **Who may be invited?** Do the invitees need to be "sophisticated", "qualified" or "accredited" investors, or have a connection to the company? Should we add a check for that?
3. **The wording below:** please rewrite as needed. Is a click-and-type signature enough, or do we need a signed share subscription agreement (and a shareholders' agreement, board resolution to allot, share certificate, register entry, PACRA filing)?
4. **Money:** how may we receive investor money (a company account, not a MoneyWise wallet)? What do we owe if an offer is declined after payment?
5. **KYC / anti-money-laundering:** what identity checks must we do before accepting an offer? (The app already has an investor KYC profile; we have not tied it to this yet.)
6. **Statements about the future:** the page shows projections from the deck (revenue 2027 to 2031, year-5 scenarios, customer numbers) and says they are projections. Are the labels enough? Should any be removed? The traction figures on the page (K2M+ processed in 6 months, net income above K70,000) must match the company's accounts.
7. **Dividends and buyback:** the wording says dividends are decided by the board and not promised, and a buyback is an option, not a right. Is that accurate to the company's constitution?
8. **Data protection:** is the privacy wording enough for the data above?
9. **Withdrawal:** the person can withdraw an offer until Blue Opus accepts it. Correct?

## The current draft wording (version `2026-11-draft-2`)

1. **What this is.** A personal agreement between you and Blue Opus (the Company) for you to buy shares in the Company. The Company is privately held and its shares are not listed or traded on any exchange.
2. **Price and your share.** The Company is valued at K7,500,000 post-money, which is its value including the money raised in this round. Your ownership is your amount divided by K7,500,000. This percentage is fixed when you sign. Later share issues can reduce it (dilution).
3. **The round.** This round raises up to K1,500,000 for shares in the Company at the K7,500,000 post-money valuation (20% of the Company in total). The Company decides which offers to accept and may close the round when it is full.
4. **Not final until the Company agrees.** Signing here is your offer. It becomes binding only when the Company countersigns it and you have paid the amount as the Company directs. The Company may decline an offer. If it does, or if you withdraw before it countersigns, nothing is owed and no money is taken here.
5. **Payment.** This page does not take payment. If your offer is accepted, the Company will tell you how to pay. Do not send money to anyone who has not confirmed it through MoneyWise.
6. **Risks you accept.** You can lose all of the money you put in. Shares in a private company are hard to sell: there is no market, and you may not be able to sell for a long time or at all. The Company may never pay dividends. The valuation is the Company's own figure and is not independently verified. Nothing here is investment, legal or tax advice.
7. **How you may get money back.** The Company intends to give shareholders a share of dividends once it is generating cash, which it expects from 2028, but dividends are decided by its board and are not promised. The Company may also offer to buy your shares back for cash at its value at that time; this is an option for the Company and for you to agree, not a right. Any figures about future revenue, value or returns in the Company's investor materials are projections, not guarantees.
8. **Records.** You will be recorded as a shareholder in the Company's share register once the Company countersigns and receives your payment. Until then you hold no shares and have no shareholder rights.
9. **Your details.** We keep your name, contact details and the details of this offer to run the agreement and to meet legal duties. Your KYC details, where needed, are handled as set out in the Investor accounts section.
10. **Law.** This agreement is governed by the laws of Zambia.

The risk notice is also shown as a footer on the page (not in the signing area). A lawyer may want it next to the sign button.

## After you approve

Blue Opus opens the page for the named investors by recording your name or firm under **Blue Opus: legal approval** (visible to platform super-admins on the page) and then sends each invitee their code.
